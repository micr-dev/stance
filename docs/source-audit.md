# Source audit

This file tracks statements that still need stronger evidence or a wording
decision before publication. It is not part of the public page.

## Water and data centers

- The oil-and-gas "psyop" attribution has no supporting source. It remains
  because Microck explicitly chose to keep it.
- Sources 3-4 support the climate and health harms associated with fossil-fuel
  energy. They do not prove the "psyop" attribution or the subjective
  superlative "most problematic."
- "One of the first widely circulated mainstream articles" is hard to prove.
  Mainstream coverage existed before the cited Washington Post article.
- The 583x and 1,996x comparisons use figures with different model,
  measurement, and water-accounting boundaries. They remain because Microck
  explicitly chose to keep them.
- Sources 12-18 cite the primary reports and datasets behind the first chart.
  The 1,400-square-mile data-center-site estimate and 25-square-mile building
  estimate remain unreproducible because the chart gives no underlying dataset
  or calculation.
- Sources 19-22 cite the studies behind the second chart. The cattle bar is a
  rounded sum of the studies' beef-cattle and dairy-cattle totals.
- The second chart's 0.5 km3 bar comes from the IEA's estimate for all data
  centers, including direct cooling plus indirect energy-supply and
  semiconductor-manufacturing use, not AI data centers alone. Its food totals
  also include green rainwater, blue surface and groundwater, and grey water,
  so the bars do not share one accounting boundary. The public caption
  discloses both problems.
- Neither chart states a reuse license, so publication still depends on
  ordinary linking and attribution rather than a confirmed redistribution
  grant.

## Hiding AI usage

- "99% of the time" is intentional hyperbole, not a measured statistic.
- The claims about the GitHub, X/Twitter biography, and website navigation
  should be checked against the live profiles before publication.

## AI-assisted tools

- The product list mixes generative AI, recommendation systems, filtering, and
  other machine-learning uses. Each named product needs a current source if the
  list stays exhaustive.
- Using an AI-mediated product is not identical to accepting AI-generated
  source code. The hypocrisy conclusion would be stronger if it addressed that
  distinction directly.
- Sources 35-37 document the Linux Foundation's support for open AI
  initiatives, Linux kernel commits using the "Assisted-by" trailer, and Linus
  Torvalds's mailing-list guidance for that trailer. They do not establish that
  every Linux installation inherently uses or integrates AI.

### Public source status

- Sources 34-50 are now approved and included in the public page. Sources
  34-48 cover every named product and category with first-party documentation.
  Sources 49-50 establish widespread AI-tool adoption among developers and
  documented AI use in production chip design.
- X's official Grok help page is discoverable through search and identifies
  Grok as an AI assistant available to X users, but currently returns HTTP 403
  to automated requests.
- Kagi Extract returned HTTP 429 during full-page extraction. The source set
  was therefore checked with Kagi Search, direct URL validation, and local
  Defuddle extraction. Fourteen of the fifteen pages returned readable
  first-party content through those checks.

These sources establish that the named ecosystems include AI features and that
AI assistance is used in software and chip development. They do not establish
that every person using those products actively uses those features, that every
device was made with AI assistance, or that refusing AI-generated code while
using a recommendation system is necessarily hypocritical.

## Vibecoding

- Karpathy's post supports the origin and wording of the term.
- Source 33 reports a $105,990 median annual wage for computer and information
  technology occupations in May 2024, compared with $49,500 for all
  occupations. It supports "one of the highest-paying fields," but not the
  rhetorical extension "if not the highest-paying field."

## Jobs

- The counterfactual claim that employers would otherwise offshore the same
  work is broader than sources 23-26. Those sources establish offshoring,
  global labor arbitrage, and the overlap between tasks exposed to technology
  and foreign labor.
- No source found establishes that employers who replace workers with AI would
  otherwise have offshored those exact jobs. That sentence should remain a
  political argument, be narrowed, or be removed.
- The Norwegian and U.S.-Europe studies support limited observed employment
  effects in the periods and datasets they studied. They do not establish that
  AI cannot displace jobs later or in other markets.

## Research limitations

- The Perplexity research connector failed before returning results because of
  an internal argument error. Kagi search, official vendor documentation,
  government data, and a Reddit counterargument scan were used instead.
